Maliyah Bass: The Playground Story That Fell Apart

The Crime Vault 247 · 2026-11-19 · Case: Maliyah Bass

In August 2020, a Houston mother appeared on camera pleading for help finding her 2-year-old daughter. She said the little girl had disappeared from an apartment complex playground in the five minutes she looked away. The community feared an abduction. But the child had already died before that missing-person call was made. How did a story told to the public come apart so quickly, and who was supposed to be protecting Maliyah?

Who she was

Maliyah Rosalie Bass was two years old. Her family remembers her as smart and full of personality, a child whose whole childhood was still ahead. Her grandmother, Rosalie Jimerson, has said that saying Maliyah's name makes her feel better, and that she wants Maliyah to know she is not forgotten. Toys were left in her memory outside the apartment building where she lived.

Maliyah lived with her mother, Sahara Ervin, and her mother's boyfriend, Travion Thompson.

What happened

On August 22, 2020, Ervin reported that she had left her daughter for only a few minutes at the apartment complex playground and that Maliyah was gone. Houston police, with help from Texas EquuSearch, began a search, and a statewide Amber Alert was issued. On August 23, Maliyah's body was found in Brays Bayou in Houston.

Police and the video note that the accounts given by the two adults were inconsistent about activity in the apartment and the last time the child was seen. A neighbor told reporters the playground story "doesn't pass the smell test," but the video is careful to say that a neighbor's doubt is not evidence. Investigators needed proof independent of anyone's statement.

The investigation

According to search records reported by the media, surveillance cameras on the night of August 21 recorded Thompson moving a large trash can toward the drainage area. That was before the missing-person report. Investigators say this timing undermined the playground account, though the footage alone did not show who caused the death.

Forensic results found that Maliyah died as a result of violence, with signs including a broken arm and bruising. According to testimony described in the investigation file, Thompson said Ervin had struck Maliyah with a hairbrush because she was crying and would not sleep. Ervin's side, in turn, accused Thompson of repeatedly beating the child. The two gave conflicting accounts, and the video states plainly that the forensic findings alone cannot establish the number of assaults or who inflicted each injury.

Thompson's record also drew attention. According to Crime Stoppers of Houston, he had five convictions since 2017. Court records cited in the video show he was convicted of assault while on probation, and that the court did not revoke his probation. In 2019 he was placed on five years of community supervision in an aggravated robbery case, and he was still under supervision when Maliyah died. The video stresses that this record shows his criminal history but does not prove authorities knew Maliyah was being abused.

The outcome in court

Both adults resolved their cases with guilty pleas. Thompson pleaded guilty. According to prosecutors, his admission about placing Maliyah's body in the drain was key evidence in his accepting the plea. He also accepted responsibility for violating the conditions of his earlier supervision. Ervin pleaded guilty in January 2022 under a cooperation agreement and was prepared to testify against Thompson; after his plea, the trial was canceled. The sentences discussed in the video are 40 years for Thompson and 20 years for Ervin. The video cautions that the difference does not mean the court found Thompson caused twice as many injuries, since sentences reflect each person's charges and plea agreement.

Ervin's attorney said she will never forgive herself and wishes she had died in her daughter's place. The case reached its legal conclusion nearly four years after Maliyah's death.

Questions that remain

Thompson's mother, speaking about what she had learned, said it was "a bunch of lies." Crime Stoppers representatives criticized the failures to protect Maliyah as early as 2020, saying her death was preventable and that "the system failed her miserably."

The video asks a larger question: how can monitoring of offenders and protection of young children improve before a tragedy rather than after it? A two-year-old cannot recognize abuse, ask for help or leave. Unexplained injuries or sudden changes in a child's behavior deserve to be taken seriously.
