One Shot, Two Victims: The Detroit Case Over a Children's Fight

The Crime Vault 247 · November 22, 2026 · Case: Aaliyah Portis

How does a fight between a 7-year-old and an 8-year-old end with a mother and her young daughter shot inside their own apartment? And when the person accused is a decorated veteran and volunteer football coach, what should a court weigh: his record, or what he is accused of doing at that door?

Who they were

Aaliyah Portis was 7 years old. By every account in this case, she had no part in the argument between the adults. Her mother, Alexis Reeves, was 33. Aaliyah's 4-year-old brother was also in the apartment that night.

The man accused of firing the shot is Aaron Matthew Wright, 32. Prosecutors say he is the father of one of the children involved in the earlier fight. His attorney described him in court as a highly decorated retired Army veteran. According to the defense, he is employed at a major automotive company and holds an associate degree in cybersecurity. He is also enrolled in a four-year university program. Wright volunteered as an assistant football coach at a Detroit high school.

What happened

Police say the day began with a fight between children, about seven and eight years old. That evening, Wright went to Reeves' apartment to confront her about it.

According to the Wayne County Prosecutor's Office, the two argued on her front step. Police say Reeves shut the door during the argument. They allege Wright forced his way inside and fired one shot. Detroit's police chief said the bullet went through Reeves' arm and struck Aaliyah near the collarbone.

Police received the 911 call at 7:03 p.m. and said officers arrived within five minutes. By then, the suspect had driven away.

Aaliyah's grandmother said bullet fragments were traveling toward her granddaughter's heart. She said doctors performed emergency open-heart surgery to save the girl's life. Both Aaliyah and her mother survived.

Afterward, the grandmother told reporters what Aaliyah's little brother said: "He said, Nana, he was trying to shoot Mommy and Aaliyah." A neighbor who rushed in to give first aid to both victims described the scene. She said she knew she had to focus because "this baby could die."

Reeves spoke publicly about the night. "A man came and shot me and my innocent daughter," she said.

The investigation

At the scene, the police chief urged the suspect to turn himself in. He described the getaway vehicle as a white GMC Terrain and said the suspect was known in the area. By the next afternoon, Detroit police announced that Wright had turned himself in at the 7th Precinct.

Wright was charged with 17 felonies. The most serious are two counts of assault with intent to murder, one for each victim. Those charges turn on intent. Prosecutors must prove both the assault and that the shooter meant to kill. A victim surviving does not erase that question.

A judge set bond at $250,000. Bond is a pretrial decision about release and risk. It is not a punishment, and it is not a finding of guilt.

The trial and outcome

At the arraignment, Wright's attorney asked for a lower bond. He argued that his client's military service, job, education, church involvement and work coaching local football made him a model citizen. In a bond hearing, those ties can help a judge decide whether a defendant is likely to follow release conditions. They do not answer what happened at the door. That is a matter of evidence and testimony.

Reeves' mother had a different response. "I just hope they throw the book at him," she said.

A community leader who stood beside the police chief that night called the shooting preventable. He said adults are expected to "emotionally regulate" and "deescalate" when children fight. Those are his words, not a legal finding.

Wright has not been convicted of anything. If convicted of assault with intent to murder, he could face life in prison. At the time of the reporting, he was due for a bond redetermination hearing, followed by a preliminary exam on November 3. The video does not cover what happened at those hearings, and we do not know the outcome of the case.

Questions that remain

  • What will the evidence show about what happened at the door, and about what the accused intended?
  • Will prosecutors be able to prove intent to kill on both counts?
  • Can a strong record of service and community work carry any weight against specific allegations of violence, or does it only matter for bond?
  • How will a 7-year-old and her 4-year-old brother recover from what they went through? The neighbors, many of them families with young children, were shaken as well.

The shooting happened in a complex where children play every day. One neighbor said, "The kids have been traumatized over this."
