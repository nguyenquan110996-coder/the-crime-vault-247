Fairfield Spirit Halloween Shooting: A Mother Lost, a Teen Charged

The Crime Vault 247 · November 22, 2026 · Case: Constance Hedges

How does a teenager who already faced charges in a shooting end up accused of murder barely a year later? And in a crowded store where seven people were shot, who was the gunman actually aiming at?

Those questions sit at the center of the October 30, 2026, shooting at a Spirit Halloween store in Fairfield, California. The shooting killed a pregnant mother of four. Police say the suspect is only 17.

Who she was

Constance Hedges was 31 years old. She was the mother of four children and was pregnant with her fifth.

Behind the name in the news reports was a woman with growing kids and a baby on the way. Her children will now face first days of school, birthdays and every milestone without her. The child she was carrying never had the chance to be born.

She was killed on the night before Halloween, inside a store in a busy shopping mall. Days later, only a few small items had been left outside the store in her memory. Her family never had the chance to say goodbye.

What happened

Seven people were hit by gunfire that Friday night. Fairfield's police chief said one of them died and the rest were taken to local hospitals. At least three were in critical condition, and the youngest of the wounded was 16.

According to what police told ABC 7, the shooting began with an argument involving the suspect inside the store. Shoppers described a few rounds of rapid gunfire, then panic. One said everyone froze, then people began running. Another said he got low and kept his head down until he could get out. A witness recalled hearing a mother crying out for her child.

Cell phone video recorded by a shopper captured the chaos as customers dropped to the ground. A woman who was outside the store the next day called it a holiday meant for children. Fairfield's mayor called it a horrific incident and promised to keep residents informed.

The investigation

Police say a school resource officer recognized the shooter on surveillance video. He was a former student. Investigators say they identified him within hours but held back the announcement so they would not compromise the search or the arrest.

On Monday, November 2, a SWAT team surrounded a home in nearby Suisun City. After several hours, the suspect surrendered peacefully. He was taken into custody in Solano County.

Police said the suspect walked into the store and spotted another customer whom investigators believe was the intended target. They said he then fired multiple rounds at that person. If the evidence confirms this account, it means violence aimed at one person killed and wounded others. Even then, that would not reduce responsibility for every other victim. So far, nothing police have released indicates that Constance had any conflict with the suspect.

The suspect's past drew immediate attention. Police say that in 2025 he faced charges for firing a gun into a car in the parking lot of a local high school. The police chief said he served a few months in juvenile hall and was then released into the community.

That history raises hard questions about how gun violence among minors is managed. It does not, on its own, show which agency, if any, fell short. Those answers would have to come from records of supervision and release conditions, not speculation.

The trial and outcome

The case has not gone to trial. On November 3, the Solano County District Attorney's Office announced the charges against the 17-year-old:

  • Two counts of murder with a multiple murder special circumstance
  • Three counts of attempted murder
  • Three counts of assault with a semi-automatic firearm
  • Gun enhancements

Prosecutors also allege a prior conviction for a violent offense, which counts as a strike under California law. The two murder counts reflect the death of Constance Hedges and the death of the unborn child she was carrying.

These are allegations, not a verdict. The teenager is presumed innocent, and on November 13 he pleaded not guilty.

His age adds another layer. Prosecutors have asked that the case be moved to adult court, but that request has not been granted. Under California law, a judge must weigh factors set out in the statute, including whether the teenager can be rehabilitated in the juvenile system. Being accused of a very serious crime does not automatically mean a teenager will be tried as an adult.

Questions that remain

Several questions are still open:

  • The intended target: Police say they believe the gunman was aiming at another customer. That is an investigative belief that still has to be tested by evidence.
  • The earlier case: The suspect's 2025 charges and his time in juvenile hall are part of the record. What supervision followed his release is not something the public knows.
  • The court's decision: A judge has yet to rule on whether this case stays in juvenile court or moves to adult court.
  • The broader question: How should the justice system assess and supervise young people with a history of gun violence before they return to the community? Second chances matter, but they have to come with a real assessment of risk.

For now, what is certain is the loss. Constance Hedges went shopping on the night before Halloween. She was 31, a mother of four with a fifth child on the way. She never came home.
